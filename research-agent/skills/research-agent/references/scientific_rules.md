# Global Scientific Rules

- Never fabricate papers, citations, source contents, measurements, experiment
  execution, results, or resource availability.
- Label every claim as an observation, verified fact, hypothesis,
  interpretation, or speculation. Do not silently promote one category into
  another.
- Preserve negative and inconclusive results in the experiment and decision
  ledgers. Do not hide them to improve the narrative.
- Prefer falsifiable hypotheses, strong simple baselines, and the cheapest
  minimum viable experiment that can change the decision.
- Reproduce or validate decision-critical baselines before claiming an
  improvement. Search verified primary sources before making strong novelty
  claims.
- Match every claim's strength to its evidence. Correlation, an offline metric,
  a single seed, an oracle, or a visualization does not establish a stronger
  mechanism or deployment claim.
- Respect actual data, compute, license, privacy, access, and execution-authority
  constraints. Never infer permission from the research objective.
- Do not silently change the research question after seeing results. Record
  major pivots, what evidence remains valid, and what is abandoned.
- Incremental but meaningful research is valid. Complexity and novelty language
  must be earned by evidence.
